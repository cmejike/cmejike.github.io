I'm **Chidera M. Ejike**, a PhD student in **Epidemiology** (minor in Biostatistics, graduate certificate in Foundations of Data Science in progress) at the University of Arizona's Mel and Enid Zuckerman College of Public Health, where I also work as a Clinical Research Coordinator in the Department of Epidemiology and Biostatistics. I trained in biotechnology before earning my MPH in Epidemiology at the University of Arizona.

My work brings together molecular biology and population health. I study post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection (long COVID), especially gastrointestinal outcomes, in the Arizona CoVHORT cohort.

Research interests
------
- Long COVID research
- Molecular and microbiome epidemiology
- Gut health and the microbiome
- Proteomics and multi-omic integration
- Food insecurity, diet and environmental exposures
- Infectious disease epidemiology and surveillance
- Social and structural determinants of health

Current work
------
- **CoVHORT-GI (R01)** - research coordination and study operations, including participant engagement, data management, and biospecimen tracking.
- **Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center** - a qualitative implementation study at the Uganda Cancer Institute on barriers to timely antibiotics for patients with cancer and neutropenic fever.
- **Southwest Center on Resilience for Climate & Health (SCORCH)** - community-engaged photovoice and scoping-review research on greenspaces and health in Southern Arizona.
- **Student Epidemiology Response Team** - lab manager for EPID 596D, supervising students in applied epidemiology response.

Before graduate school I coordinated molecular biology and microbiome research at the DNA Learning Center Nigeria, where I led the Back-to-Science Initiative (BATOSCIN) and helped train about 200 secondary-school students in foundational molecular biology.
